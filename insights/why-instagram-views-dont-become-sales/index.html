Why your Instagram views are not becoming sales

Your reel got 20,000 views. Your inbox is still quiet. Before you blame the algorithm, look at the buying journey.

Views are attention, not purchase intent

Someone can watch a video because it is funny, surprising or useful without needing what you sell. Reach tells you that content travelled. It does not tell you whether the right buyer understood your offer or trusted you enough to take the next step.

For a Lagos fashion business, a trending video may attract people who enjoy the outfit but cannot buy it, live outside your delivery area or do not understand how to order. The first question is not “How do we get more views?” It is “Who are we trying to move, and what decision should they make next?”

Diagnose the customer journey

Audit the journey from first impression to payment. Look at each stage independently:

  1. Audience: Does your content speak to a recognisable customer and buying situation?
  2. Offer: Can a new visitor tell what you sell, who it is for and what makes it different?
  3. Trust: Do your photos, reviews, delivery terms, business information and responses reduce uncertainty?
  4. Action: Is it obvious whether to DM, click a link, visit a shop or request a quote?
  5. Follow-up: When a customer asks for a price, does the conversation move forward?

For example, “DM for price” may create friction for buyers who want to compare options quickly. For a custom printing business, a simple price range or minimum-order explanation may qualify enquiries more effectively than hiding every detail.

Fix the message before buying more ads

Write down one customer problem, one credible promise, one reason to believe and one next step. Then build content around that sequence.

Instead of “We sell premium gift boxes,” try “Need 50 staff welcome boxes before your induction? See our customisation options and request a quote.”

The second message is more specific about the customer, occasion and action. It may attract fewer casual likes and more relevant enquiries.

Track the numbers that matter

Profile visitsDid the content make people curious enough to investigate?
Qualified enquiriesDid people with a relevant need reach out?
Enquiry-to-order rateAre conversations turning into sales?
Average order valueWhat is the commercial value of a typical sale?
Repeat purchasesAre customers satisfied enough to return?

Track these for several weeks before changing everything at once. If views rise but qualified enquiries do not, investigate the audience, offer and call to action. If enquiries rise but orders do not, investigate response speed, pricing, trust and follow-up.

A seven-day action plan

  1. Interview three recent buyers about why they chose you.
  2. Ask three people who enquired but did not buy what stopped them.
  3. Rewrite your bio and offer so a stranger understands them in ten seconds.
  4. Publish one post answering a genuine buying objection.
  5. Publish one clear product or service explainer with a direct next step.
  6. Review your DM or WhatsApp response process.
  7. Compare qualified enquiries and orders, not just reach.

Do not expect one week to prove a marketing strategy. Use it to discover where the customer journey breaks.
