How to build a marketing plan for a Nigerian business

A marketing plan is not a calendar full of posts. It is a set of decisions about who you want to reach, why they should choose you and how you will earn their business.

A marketing plan is not a calendar full of posts. It is a set of decisions about who you want to reach, why they should choose you and how you will earn their business.

Start with one business outcome

Choose a clear commercial problem. “We want to be more visible” is too broad. “We want more qualified wholesale enquiries in Lagos over the next quarter” gives your team something useful to plan around.

Decide what success means before choosing channels. Possible objectives include enquiries, first purchases, repeat orders, bookings or better lead quality. Pick one primary objective and a few supporting indicators.

Understand the buying situation

Who buys from you, who influences the decision and what triggers the purchase? A parent choosing after-school lessons behaves differently from an HR manager buying training for 40 staff. Both may discover you online, but their questions, approval process and objections differ.

  • Customer: Who is the decision-maker and who else influences the purchase?
  • Situation: What event or frustration creates the need?
  • Motivation: What outcome are they buying, beyond the product?
  • Barrier: What might stop them from trusting or choosing you?
  • Evidence: What can you show that reduces that uncertainty?

Interview actual customers. Avoid replacing customer evidence with assumptions about what “Nigerians” supposedly like.

Position your business before promoting it

Complete this sentence: “For [specific customer] who needs [specific outcome], we provide [offer] because [credible difference].”

For growing retail businesses that need consistent branded packaging, we supply practical packaging options with clear ordering and delivery arrangements.

Positioning is a choice. If you try to appeal to everyone, your content often becomes generic. Make your strongest difference easy to recognise.

Choose channels by customer behaviour

ChannelUseful whenWhat to measure
Google SearchCustomers already know what they need and are comparing optionsQualified enquiries and conversion
Instagram / TikTokDemonstration, storytelling and discovery influence buyingRelevant profile visits, enquiries and orders
WhatsAppConversation and follow-up are important to closing salesResponse time and enquiry-to-order rate
LinkedIn / direct outreachDecision-makers buy for companies or teamsRelevant meetings, proposals and wins
Offline partnershipsLocal trust and referrals are powerfulIntroductions and resulting business

You do not need to be active everywhere. Start with the channels your customers already use to research and buy.

Build a simple 90-day execution plan

  1. Days 1–15: Interview customers, review existing results, clarify positioning and identify the main bottleneck.
  2. Days 16–30: Improve the offer, website or WhatsApp journey and prepare two to three message tests.
  3. Days 31–60: Run focused experiments, record spend and track enquiries or sales quality.
  4. Days 61–90: Review what worked, stop weak activities and allocate effort to the strongest opportunities.

Set a budget you can afford to test without relying on immediate returns. Include creative production, response capacity and fulfilment—not just ad spend.

Measure the business, not just the content

At minimum, record how people found you, how many qualified enquiries arrived, how many became customers, the value of orders and your campaign costs. Use consistent definitions so you can compare one month with the next.

A useful plan is not the thickest document. It is one your team can actually execute, review and improve.
